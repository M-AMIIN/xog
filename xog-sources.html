THE ARCHIVE

Sources

Eleven entries stand established as of this writing; the number grows as the Fragments board weaves, and each new entry arrives here with its own article and its chain of evidence intact. Every entry carries forward a specific argument, through direct record, oral inheritance, or deliberate counter-narrative. Every card states who recorded the source, when, and for whom, because provenance shapes meaning. Each entry here is established: it stood on three or more independent sources before it was filed, and claims still short of that standard work their way up on the Fragments board. These are the pillars. Read them, and the archive becomes defensible.

CHRONICLE

Futuh al-Habasha

Shihab al-Din Ahmad (Arab Faqih) | written 1530s · Cairo ms. 1559

The only eyewitness chronicle of the Adal conquest of Ethiopia, written by a companion of Ahmad Gurey who names every commander, every battle, every town taken. The second half has never been found.

Recorded on campaign, 1530s · for the Muslim record of the conquest

HISTORICAL TEXT

Rihla

Ibn Battuta | 1331

The Moroccan traveler's record of Mogadishu in the fourteenth century. Not a history written for historians, but a merchant's observations on trade, wealth, and the ordering of a coastal city.

Dictated to Ibn Juzayy, Fez, 1355 · for the Marinid court

ORAL TRADITION

Deelley

The Burden — A Chain Debate

A silsilad: one poet opens, others answer, all bound to the same alliteration in D. The Deelley is the most famous of these chain debates, dozens of poets arguing nation, clan, and conscience in verse. A parliament conducted in poetry.
